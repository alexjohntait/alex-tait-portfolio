# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Art directors at agencies and brands, and editorial commissioners at magazines and publications. Both scan for illustration talent from a desk, reviewing multiple candidates. Primary job: assess range, craft, and fit within 3 minutes. Decision: reach out or move on. They arrive via referral, social link, or direct search.

## Product Purpose

Alex Tait's portfolio. Alex is an illustrator and motion designer working between London and Bath, represented by Jelly. The site shows commercial, editorial, and personal work to win commissions. Success is a creative director recognising the work and reaching out, or going through Jelly. The work speaks first; the site gets out of its way. Contact: alex@alexjohntait.com, Instagram @alexjohntait.

## Brand Personality

Bold, playful, precise. The work is vivid, character-driven, and built on movement (Alex describes a simple style focused on expression and movement). The site should feel like meeting Alex: direct, warm, confident — never fussy.

## Anti-references

- Generic Squarespace illustrator portfolios: safe white grid, centred name, pastel thumbnail tiles, interchangeable with hundreds of others.
- Brutalist noise: aggressive overlapping type, random rotations, legibility sacrificed for style.
- Editorial-magazine default (the AI lane): display serif italic + mono labels + broadsheet ruled grid + warm cream background. Overused. Not this.
- Corporate cold: agency-polished, colour-drained, feels like a SaaS product page.

## Design Principles

1. Work first. The illustrations are the argument. Show them fully and immediately — no design moves that delay or hide them.
2. Confident restraint. The site has a clear voice without competing with the colour and energy of the illustrations for attention.
3. Clarity at pace. Art directors scan fast. Category, client, and tone must be readable at a glance.
4. Personality without performance. Warmth comes through detail and considered choices, not performed quirkiness.
5. One visual world. Grid, detail pages, and navigation feel like a single considered thing — not assembled from parts.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Full keyboard navigation. Meaningful alt text on all illustrations. Reduced-motion support for all transitions and hover effects.
